01 / The Brief

From description to e-commerce transaction.

Frameless had a brochure site. It described the product, listed the range, and asked people to get in touch. Everything after that happened by phone, by email, or not at all.

The job was to turn it into a shop, and to do it without losing what made the product worth buying in the first place. Architectural glass is a considered purchase. It is also intensely visual, and almost impossible to sell from a static photograph.

04 / The Challenge

A shop is a different animal to a brochure.

Moving from brochure to e-commerce isn't a redesign with a checkout bolted on. Product data has to be structured, variants have to make sense to someone who isn't a glazier, delivery and lead times have to be stated plainly, and every page that used to end in "contact us" has to end somewhere better.

We rebuilt the site bespoke rather than dropping the range into a theme, because the product range doesn't behave like a normal catalogue and a template would have forced it to.

05 / What we did

01 / Build

Bespoke, with a 3D hero

A full redesign and custom build, opening on a 3D animated sequence. Glass reads as nothing much in a still photograph, it needs light moving across it before it looks like the thing you'd pay for.

02 / Optimise

Search mapped to real demand

Page-level keyword optimisation across the range, structured so that each product and product type has a page capable of ranking on its own terms rather than everything funnelling through the homepage.

03 / Show it moving

Social, because product is visual

Once the site was live it was obvious the range deserved to be seen rather than described. We moved into short-form video on Instagram and Facebook, showing installations and mechanisms in motion.

04 / Maintain

Live site maintenance

Ongoing maintenance, updates and support since launch, alongside the marketing programme, so the shop keeps working rather than slowly rotting after handover.
