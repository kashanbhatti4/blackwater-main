The Brief

Website design and campaign setup.

Electrical inspection isn't a service people browse for. They search for it once, usually because a landlord, an insurer or a sale has told them they need a certificate, and they call whoever answers first.

We built the website and ran the search campaigns that feed it. That combination is the point: an ad is only as good as what it lands on, and a site is only worth building if the right people reach it. Both sides were ours, so neither could blame the other.

What we did

Separate

Split the account by job type

Inspection, commercial and domestic wiring run as separate campaigns rather than one catch-all. Different buyers, different urgency, different value, and impossible to read the account without separating them.

Measure

Tracked enquiries, not clicks

Conversion tracking set against submitted enquiry forms rather than page views or arrivals, so the bidding was steered by something with commercial meaning from the start.

Prune

Continuous search term pruning

Ongoing negative keyword work against job-seeker, DIY and supplier queries, the traffic that looks like demand in a report and never becomes a job.

Concentrate

Backed what was working

Once the inspection category proved itself, budget followed the evidence rather than being spread evenly across categories for the sake of coverage.

Landing experience

Most of this demand arrives on a phone.

Someone has been told they need a certificate and they're looking it up on the spot. We built the site around that moment: service and next step obvious within seconds, the enquiry form reachable without hunting, and no interstitial standing between an ad click and the thing the click was for.

Because we built the site as well as the campaigns, the landing pages could be shaped around what the search data was showing rather than the campaigns being bent to fit a site somebody else had already finished.
